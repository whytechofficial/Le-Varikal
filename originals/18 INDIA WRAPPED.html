India 2025 Wrapped

A chronicle of the crises that shaped us this year.

LE VARIKAL

Voice. Vision. Verse.

Introduction

2025 produced political turmoil, environmental rollbacks, rights violations, economic stress and security crises. Many faded from headlines within days. This wrap restores them to public memory—because forgetting benefits power, not people.


Attention is political. Memory is resistance.

01 / Security

Pahalgam Attack and Aftermath

Kashmir Conflict

The April 22 attack killed at least 26 civilians in Kashmir. India blamed Pakistan for supporting LeT-linked militants. Diplomatic ties collapsed, the Indus Waters Treaty was suspended, and cross-border strikes followed. Tourist arrivals in Kashmir fell by over 50 percent compared to 2024. The crisis exposed long-standing security and intelligence vulnerabilities, while ordinary civilians paid the price.

02 / Democracy

Vote Chori Allegations

Elections

In 2025, allegations emerged of vote manipulation and irregularities in election results in key states. Opposition parties demanded investigations into Electronic Voting Machine data, booth-wise discrepancies and differential turnout patterns. The Election Commission’s responses were limited, and no comprehensive public hearings were conducted, deepening mistrust in the electoral process.

03 / Human Rights

Manipur Was Abandoned

Northeast India

Manipur entered 2025 carrying the wounds of nearly two years of unchecked violence. The Union Government imposed President’s Rule only after the elected government failed to restore even basic law and order. Even under President’s Rule, militarisation increased while rehabilitation, reconciliation and accountability remained painfully slow. Manipur became a reminder of what happens when a crisis fades from national memory.

04 / Agriculture

Farmers’ Protests Returned

Protest

Farmers returned to protest sites across multiple states demanding a legal guarantee for Minimum Support Price, rollback of trade liberalisation measures and relief from rising input costs. Detentions, barricades and internet shutdowns echoed the 2020–21 protests, exposing how unresolved agrarian distress keeps resurfacing despite repeated assurances.

05 / Environment

Heatwaves and Climate Deaths

Climate Change

2025 witnessed one of the deadliest heatwave seasons in recent history. Temperatures crossed survivability thresholds across North and Central India. Outdoor workers, the elderly and the urban poor were most affected. Heat action plans proved inadequate, revealing that climate collapse is already a public health emergency—not a future scenario.

06 / Pollution

Environmental Decline and Pollution

Urban

Delhi and several northern cities spent extended periods in the “severe” AQI category. Water bodies showed alarming contamination levels. Large infrastructure and mining projects received expedited clearances despite ecological risks. Environmental regulation weakened even as pollution reached crisis levels.

07 / Conservation

Aravalli Hills Redefined

Land Rights

The government introduced a definition recognising only landforms 100 metres above surrounding ground as Aravalli Hills. Experts estimate that over 90 percent of the range lost protection under this rule, opening vast areas to mining, deforestation and groundwater depletion across North India.

08 / Society

Nagpur Communal Violence

Communalism

Nagpur erupted in communal violence after a right-wing mobilisation demanded the removal of Mughal emperor Aurangzeb’s tomb and rumours spread that a holy book had been desecrated. Crowds clashed with police; vehicles and buildings were set on fire; stones and petrol bombs were thrown. Curfews were imposed across multiple areas.

09 / Culture

Religious Places and History as Flashpoints

Judiciary

Beyond Nagpur, several towns saw tensions over temples, mosques, tombs and historical reinterpretations. Courts, police and local administrations increasingly became arbiters of religious disputes, accelerating the politicisation of faith and history in everyday governance.

10 / Federalism

Ladakh Protests and Wangchuk’s Arrest

Statehood

Ladakh witnessed mass protests demanding statehood and Sixth Schedule protections. Clashes in Leh on September 24 led to four deaths and dozens of injuries. Internet services were shut down. Activist Sonam Wangchuk was arrested under the National Security Act, and his NGO lost its FCRA licence—raising serious concerns about dissent suppression in a strategically sensitive region.

11 / Digital Rights

DPDP Act and Digital Rights

Privacy

The Digital Personal Data Protection Act came into force in 2025 amid strong criticism from privacy advocates. The law grants limited user rights while providing broad exemptions for the state, intelligence agencies and private entities. With no independent data regulator and weak safeguards, critics warned that surveillance would expand with little accountability.

12 / Surveillance

Expansion of Surveillance and Internet Shutdowns

Civil Liberties

Facial recognition systems, predictive policing tools and centralised databases expanded rapidly. Oversight remained minimal. India continued to lead the world in internet shutdowns, often imposed during protests, exams or communal tensions. Shutdowns increasingly became a first response, disrupting livelihoods, education and emergency communication.

13 / Media

Press Freedom Under Pressure

Journalism

Journalists faced arrests, raids, legal harassment and online intimidation—especially while reporting on communal violence, Kashmir, corruption and environmental conflicts. Self-censorship deepened as legal risks increased, narrowing the space for independent journalism.

14 / Law

Criminal Law Overhaul and Civil Liberties

Justice

The rollout of new criminal law codes continued, with lawyers and civil rights groups warning of expanded police powers, diluted safeguards and vague definitions open to abuse. Implementation gaps and lack of judicial preparedness raised fears of arbitrary detention and procedural injustice.

15 / Economy

Unemployment and Informal Work Crisis

Jobs

Youth unemployment and underemployment remained persistently high. Gig work insecurity, contractualisation and stagnant wages defined the labour market. Despite headline GDP growth, job creation lagged sharply, exposing the widening gap between economic data and lived reality.

16 / Education

Education System Strain and Student Distress

Students

Universities and exam systems faced allegations of paper leaks, evaluation irregularities, fee hikes and campus policing. Student protests were met with suspensions, FIRs and disciplinary action, deepening alienation among young citizens.

17 / Industrial

Ethanol Plant Protests Turn Violent

Rajasthan

In Hanumangarh, Rajasthan, protests against a proposed ethanol plant escalated into arson, police action and mass detentions. Locals feared groundwater depletion, land loss and contamination. The unrest reflected growing distrust of rushed industrial projects in ecologically fragile regions.

18 / Tragedy

Air India Crash

Disaster

Air India Flight 171, a Boeing 787-8 Dreamliner, crashed seconds after takeoff from Ahmedabad, killing 241 people onboard and 19 on the ground. Only one passenger survived. Preliminary findings revealed both engines lost thrust after fuel control switches moved to the OFF position, but investigators have yet to explain why.

19 / Judiciary

Shoe Thrown at CJI Gavai

Supreme Court

A senior advocate attempted to throw a shoe at Chief Justice Gavai during a Supreme Court hearing. The unprecedented incident exposed the intensity of political and ideological polarisation entering even the highest judicial spaces.

20 / Federalism

The Southern Fiscal Blockade

South India

In early 2025, Chief Ministers from Tamil Nadu, Kerala, and Karnataka staged an unprecedented joint sit-in at Jantar Mantar. They protested the Union’s refusal to release disaster relief funds and the shrinking share of tax devolutions. The protest highlighted the widening "North-South divide," with southern leaders accusing the Centre of penalising their states for better development indices.

21 / Urban Crisis

Bengaluru’s ‘Day Zero’ Riots

Water Crisis

Bengaluru’s water crisis hit a breaking point in May, leading to what locals called "Water Apartheid." While tanker mafias charged exorbitant rates in gated communities, peri-urban areas and slum settlements faced dry taps for weeks. Tensions boiled over into skirmishes between residents and water tanker operators.

22 / Representation

The Delimitation Anxiety

Democracy

As the freeze on parliamentary delimitation neared its end, anxiety gripped South India. Civil society groups and political parties launched campaigns fearing that the 2026 delimitation would punish southern states for successful population control by reducing their Lok Sabha seats. The silence of the Union Government on guaranteeing a non-punitive formula turned this demographic concern into an existential political crisis.

23 / Rehabilitation

Wayanad Rehabilitation Failures

Kerala

A year after the devastating landslides, survivors in Wayanad staged protests alleging that promised rehabilitation packages had not materialized. Like Joshimath before it, the region saw temporary shelters turning permanent, with the ecologically sensitive zone being reopened for tourism and construction despite expert warnings.

24 / Technology

The AI Layoff Wave

Labor

The IT corridors of Hyderabad, Bengaluru, and Pune witnessed the first mass mobilisations of white-collar workers. Major tech firms executed silent layoffs attributed to "AI restructuring," leaving thousands of mid-level employees jobless without severance transparency. The crisis shattered the "middle-class safety" myth of the IT sector.

25 / Social Justice

The Caste Census Standoff

Politics

The demand for a nationwide Caste Census became the year’s most volatile political football. While several states initiated their own surveys, the Union Government’s refusal to release updated national data led to confusing, contradictory policy implementations. In Bihar and Maharashtra, this led to massive counter-mobilisations.

26 / Environment

Sterlite 2.0: The Coastal Protests

Tamil Nadu

In coastal Tamil Nadu, renewed protests against the expansion of a petrochemical project were met with heavy-handed police action. Villagers cited rising cancer rates and loss of marine life. The administration invoked Section 144 for months, effectively silencing dissent and preventing media access to the affected fishing hamlets.

27 / Healthcare

The Doctor Safety Movement

Medical

Following another brutal assault on a resident doctor in a government hospital in Kolkata, the medical fraternity launched a nationwide strike that paralyzed healthcare for days. The demand for a Central Protection Act was met with assurances but no legislation. The strike highlighted the collapsing infrastructure of public health.
